- Replace manual, disconnected processes with integrated systems.

- Connect legacy systems to modern applications.

- Provide specialist talent quickly.

### Emotional/organisational value

## 4. How the business model shapes engineering work

| Business reality | Engineering implication |
|---|---|
| Client projects with fixed timelines | Scope control, clear requirements, milestone delivery |
| Multiple ERP platforms | Integration work, data mapping, vendor-specific constraints |
| Legacy environments | Maintenance, backward compatibility, careful migrations |
| Finance/enterprise clients | Auditability, access control, data integrity, reporting accuracy |
| Small teams | Generalists who can move across frontend, backend, and integration |
| International clients | English documentation, time-zone collaboration, clear async communication |
| Consulting margin pressure | Reuse, standardisation, avoid gold-plating |
| Product (FOOMA) plus services | Balance product roadmap with client-specific customisation |

**Key insight for the case:** A consultancy values a solution that can be delivered predictably and maintained by a small team more than a technically impressive but over-engineered design.

## 6. Competitive analysis (SWOT)

### Strengths
- Multi-platform ERP capability.
- Regional delivery track record.
- Own product (FOOMA) alongside services.
- Lean, senior-heavy consulting background.
- Broad technology exposure for juniors.

### Weaknesses
- Small size limits capacity for very large programmes.
- Breadth of stack can dilute depth.
- Brand recognition lower than large consultancies or global SIs.
- Public information about engineering practices is limited.

### Opportunities
- AI/automation demand in enterprise finance and operations.
- Odoo and Dynamics growth in mid-market Indonesia.
- Regional expansion (Singapore, NZ, US).
- Product-led revenue through FOOMA.
- Talent shortage in ERP integration.

### Threats
- ERP vendors expanding direct implementation services.
- Price competition from offshore and local boutiques.
- Client budget cycles and economic slowdown.
- Talent attrition to larger tech companies.
- Delivery risk from overcommitting on scope.

**Use in interview:** Mention one strength and one honest challenge, not a generic SWOT recital.

## 8. Five Whys: why a consultancy needs strong juniors

1. **Why hire fresh graduates?** To build a pipeline of consultants/developers at sustainable cost.
2. **Why not only seniors?** Seniors are scarce and expensive; projects need scalable delivery capacity.
3. **Why does delivery capacity matter?** Client projects have fixed timelines and margins.
4. **Why does margin matter?** It funds certifications, sales, and product development.
5. **Why does that affect the assessment?** They select people who can learn quickly, communicate with clients, and become productive without constant supervision.

**Implication:** Demonstrate learning speed, structured thinking, and communication — not just current technical knowledge.

## 9. Interview-ready lines

- "Your model combines ERP implementation, custom development, and a product like FOOMA. That means a programmer here needs to understand both the client's process and the underlying data flow, not just write features."
- "Because projects are client-facing and time-boxed, I would prioritise a clear scope, a thin end-to-end delivery, and documentation that another consultant can pick up."
- "I am interested in the consulting model because it exposes me to different industries and forces me to explain technical decisions to non-technical stakeholders."

Use at most one of these per conversation. Do not recite all three.

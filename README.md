## Five findings that change preparation

1. **The role is broader than "web developer."** The archived Fresh Graduate Programmer advertisement lists **Java, PHP, Angular, .NET, and Flutter**, and asks for "basic programming concepts, not just `SELECT * FROM table_name`." Marchel's Next.js/TypeScript strength is useful but not sufficient by itself.
2. **Public materials position the company as an IT consultancy with service and product offerings.** It advertises Oracle/Odoo/Microsoft Dynamics implementation, custom development, FOOMA and talent services; revenue mix is not public. Clarify the business problem, scope, constraints and trade-offs before coding.
3. **English is a working requirement, not decoration.** The company delivers to Indonesia, Singapore, New Zealand, and the US. The assessment is in English. Clear, simple explanations beat sophisticated vocabulary.
4. **Prepare for Criteria's standard 50-question, 15-minute CCAT**, based on the name in the candidate brief. This is `INFERRED`, not a confirmed Amber format; no invitation document has been inspected.
5. **The "Programmer Business Case" format is unknown.** It may be a written case, a live discussion, a small coding task, SQL, pseudocode, or system design. Prepare a transferable method rather than guessing one format.

## Honesty rules used throughout

Evidence-label definitions and access limits: `sources/README.md`. Final checks and remaining verification limits: `sources/verification_report.md`.

- Company facts carry a source and a confidence label.
- Unknowns are labelled `UNKNOWN`, not filled with plausible invention.
- Candidate experience is drawn from the CV and portfolio only. No employer, project, or skill is invented.
- Practice questions are original. They are *inspired by* public CCAT descriptions, not copied from Criteria or leaked material.
- Documentary exposure is not a validated competency score. Mock reports stay blank until actual attempts; personal incident prompts remain unfinished rather than fabricated.

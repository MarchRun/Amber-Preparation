# Behavioural Answers — Evidence Before STAR

**Purpose:** Build ten honest answers without inventing conflict, failure or feedback.
**Created / last updated:** 31 October 2026
**Status:** Five documented-role drafts plus five incident prompts. Bracketed fields must be supplied by Marchel before use.

STAR = Situation, Task, Action, Result; add a brief learning. A CV responsibility supports a role description, not a detailed incident or a measured outcome. Do not fabricate a complete story simply to fill the framework.

## 1. Teamwork — documented draft

"At Bappenas, I worked as a backend developer intern in a four-person internship team. My responsibility was backend features in PHP, CodeIgniter and MySQL. I coordinated with team members and cross-department stakeholders so implementation aligned with institutional requirements and protocols. We used Agile development, Jira and GitLab. That supports a concrete account of my team role. To make this a full STAR story, I would add one real feature, the dependency on another role and the actual acceptance outcome. I would not claim I led the team or deployed the entire system."

Follow-up: What did another teammate need from you? Emphasise your action, not "we" for every sentence.

## 2. Leadership — documented draft

"For Kibang Soil Tracker, my role was project manager. The system monitored village soil conditions, including pH, temperature and moisture. I led planning and prepared specifications, budget, risk and testing documentation. I also contributed to frontend development, database integration and sensor testing. That gave me responsibilities beyond coding, but it was project experience rather than professional management of engineers. For an incident example, I would explain one actual planning decision and how the team responded. I would describe the result without inventing schedule or cost savings."

Follow-up: Which planning artifact changed a decision? Emphasise a verifiable artifact.

## 3. Learning — documented draft

"Iterary was a library management PWA, and my role was cloud engineer. I configured the server environment, application deployment and supporting services. I also established an automated deployment workflow and configured traffic routing. Docker, Google Cloud, MySQL and Redis appear in the project stack. I can use that project to explain a real deployment task I learned. Before presenting it as a learning story, I would identify the exact topic I did not know, the reference I used and how I verified the result. Those details are not supplied by the portfolio alone."

Follow-up: What observation proved the deployment worked? Emphasise validation rather than a technology list.

## 4. Ambiguity — documented draft

"Simonika is listed in my portfolio as an internal ICT infrastructure monitoring project. My backend role used PHP, CodeIgniter and MySQL. I implemented functionality based on departmental requirements and SOPs and connected frontend workflows with database operations. That is relevant to translating an institutional process into software. For an ambiguity story, I would need to add a real unclear requirement, who clarified it and what changed. I should not claim an invented disagreement or assert that Simonika is either identical to or separate from the internship system without confirming the relationship."

Follow-up: What requirement was actually unclear? Emphasise clarification and traceability.

## 5. Coordination — documented draft

"As Secretary General of KMB Dhirasena ITERA, I supported organisational development, structures and strategic decision-making. I coordinated with members and external stakeholders. This gives me a non-coding example of responsibility and communication. To answer a question about a specific outcome, I would choose one actual activity and name my contribution, rather than turn the role title into a general claim of success. I would also distinguish this organisational role from managing a paid software team. The useful lesson should come from the real incident I choose."

Follow-up: What decision or handoff did your coordination improve? Emphasise ownership without exaggerated authority.

## 6. Conflict — complete a real incident

"In [actual project], we disagreed about [specific issue]. My responsibility was [scope]. I first [listening/verification action actually taken]. We compared [real criteria] and agreed [decision]. The observed result was [verifiable outcome]. I learned [specific lesson]."

Follow-up: What did you change your mind about? No conflict is documented; if none is suitable, say so and offer a hypothetical approach explicitly.

## 7. Failure — complete a real incident

"I expected [outcome], but [actual failure] happened. My part was [action/omission]. I [real recovery action]. The result was [actual state], with [remaining limitation]. Next time I would [concrete prevention]."

Follow-up: What evidence links your action to the failure? Do not invent a failed deployment, missing test suite or production outage.

## 8. Deadline / prioritisation — complete a real incident

"We had [real deadline] for [scope]. I identified [must-have] and [deferred work], discussed the trade-off with [actual person/role], and [action]. We delivered [actual outcome]. I learned [lesson about scope or escalation]."

Follow-up: What did you explicitly not deliver? No deadline incident is recorded in the source documents.

## 9. Feedback — complete a real incident

"I received feedback on [specific artifact]. Initially I understood it as [actual reaction]. I asked [clarifying question], changed [specific part] and checked [evidence]. The result was [observed outcome]."

Follow-up: Which feedback did you not adopt, and why? Do not manufacture a senior review conversation.

## 10. Mistake — complete a real incident

"I made [specific mistake] while responsible for [scope]. I noticed it through [observation], informed [appropriate person] and [fix]. I then added [real check/process]. I would handle [remaining issue] differently now."

Follow-up: How do you know the fix addressed the cause? A hypothetical debugging pattern is not a personal bug history.

**Indonesian reminder:** Bagian dalam kurung bukan fakta. Isi dari pengalaman asli atau nyatakan bahwa jawaban merupakan pendekatan hipotetis. Hasil kualitatif yang benar lebih baik daripada angka karangan.

## 2. Before the assessment

### 48 hours before
- Confirm the Google Meet link works.
- Test camera, microphone, speakers, and screen sharing in a test call.
- Check laptop power and charger.
- Identify a quiet room with a neutral background and good lighting.
- Prepare a backup internet connection (phone hotspot).
- Print or open the cheat sheet on a second device if permitted.
- Prepare water, notebook, pen, and a simple clock/timer.

### 12 hours before
- Re-read the fact sheet and cheat sheet only. No new topics.
- Prepare clothes.
- Charge all devices.
- Sleep at least 7 hours.
- Set two alarms.

### 1 hour before
- Eat a light meal.
- Avoid heavy caffeine if it increases anxiety.
- Close unnecessary apps and browser tabs.
- Silence phone notifications; keep the phone nearby for hotspot/emergency.
- Join the lobby early.

## 3. During the assessment

### General conduct
1. Greet the interviewer in English.
2. Keep the camera on unless told otherwise.
3. Look at the camera when speaking, not only at the screen.
4. Speak slowly and finish sentences.
5. If you do not understand, ask a precise clarification question.
6. Do not read scripted answers; use notes only if expressly permitted.
7. Do not claim experience you do not have.
8. Keep a glass of water within reach.
9. If technical failure occurs, state it clearly and use the chat/phone fallback.

### CCAT-specific
- Read the on-screen instructions before starting.
- Confirm the number of questions and time limit if shown.
- Do not spend more than ~45 seconds on any item.
- Answer every item; there is no wrong-answer penalty in the standard CCAT.
- Use scratch paper only if permitted by the actual rules.
- If the platform is forward-only, accept that you cannot return.

### Business-case-specific
- Ask clarifying questions before proposing a solution.
- State assumptions explicitly.
- Structure the answer: context → requirements → approach → trade-offs → next steps.
- If coding is requested, clarify language and scope, then write a thin working solution.
- If you run out of time, summarise what is done and what remains.

| Searching for answers during a proctored test | Possible disqualification |
| Sharing test content afterward | May violate confidentiality |
| Overstating Java/.NET/Flutter experience | Erodes trust immediately |
| Arguing with the examiner | Damages collaboration signal |

## 5. If something goes wrong

| Problem | Response |
|---|---|
| Internet drops | Reconnect; if not possible, use phone hotspot; message recruiter |
| Camera fails | State it; continue with audio; offer to reconnect |
| Mic fails | Use chat; call in by phone if number provided |
| Power outage | Switch to charged laptop/phone; notify recruiter |
| You do not understand a question | "Sorry, could you repeat the question more slowly?" |
| You do not know an answer | "I have not used that in production. My closest experience is X. I would approach it by Y." |
| You feel panicked | Pause, breathe once, ask for a moment, continue |

## 6. After the assessment

- Thank the interviewer.
- Record non-confidential reflections, not protected assessment questions or employer data.
- Send a short thank-you email within 24 hours.
- Update `../PROGRESS_TRACKER.md` with what happened.
- Do not obsess over answers you cannot change.

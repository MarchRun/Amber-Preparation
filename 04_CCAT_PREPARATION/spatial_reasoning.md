# Spatial Reasoning

**Purpose:** Improve the hardest-to-fake CCAT category with simple, repeatable methods.
**Created:** 31 October 2026
**Last updated:** 31 October 2026

## 1. What is tested

Spatial reasoning measures the ability to visualise objects, make spatial judgments, and solve problems.

Formats:
- Shape series: what comes next?
- 3×3 matrix: which figure completes the pattern?
- Odd one out
- Rotation and reflection
- Paper folding and hole punching
- Cube nets / opposite faces
- Figure comparison

---

## 2. Core method: track one feature at a time

Do not try to hold the whole image in your head. Track features separately:

| Feature | Question to ask |
|---|---|
| Position | Where is it? |
| Orientation | Which way is it facing? |
| Rotation | How many degrees? Clockwise or counter-clockwise? |
| Reflection | Is it mirrored? |
| Fill | Solid, hollow, shaded? |
| Count | How many sides/dots/lines? |
| Size | Growing, shrinking, constant? |
| Arrangement | Inside, outside, touching, separate? |

Write the rule in words: "The arrow rotates 90° clockwise and the dot moves one corner clockwise."

## 3. Rotation rules

### Clockwise
- North → East → South → West → North.
- 90° clockwise = quarter turn right.
- 180° = upside down.
- 270° clockwise = 90° counter-clockwise.

### Counter-clockwise
- North → West → South → East.

### Letters under rotation
These are idealised block-letter approximations. Actual fonts may not map exactly; graphical questions must be judged from the supplied drawing, not font-independent letter rules.
- `b` rotated 180° → `q`.
- `p` rotated 180° → `d`.
- `M` rotated 180° → `W`.
- `N` rotated 180° → `N` (with a different orientation; be careful).
- `6` rotated 180° → `9`.

### Reflection vs. rotation
- A reflection reverses chirality (left/right swap).
- A rotation preserves chirality.
- If a shape has an asymmetric detail (a dot on one side), use it to distinguish.

## 4. Paper folding

### Method
1. Count the number of layers after each fold.
2. A small punch away from folds and edges creates one separate hole per layer if unfolded holes do not overlap.
3. Unfold step by step, mirroring the hole across each fold line.
4. Check symmetry: holes are mirrored across every fold.

### Common results
| Folds | Layers | Holes from one punch |
|---|---:|---:|
| Fold in half once | 2 | 2 |
| Fold in half twice | 4 | 4 |
| Fold in half three times | 8 | 8 |
| Fold corner to corner once | 2 | 2 |

**Trap:** A punch near a fold line can merge holes. Read the position carefully.

## 5. Cube nets

A cube has 6 faces; opposite faces do not share an edge in the folded cube. Read the exact net before identifying them.

### Standard cross net
```
    [1]
[2] [3] [4] [5]
    [6]
```
Opposite pairs depend on the layout. In this cross:
- 1 and 6 are opposite.
- 2 and 4 are opposite.
- 3 and 5 are opposite.

### Method
1. Pick a face.
2. For the displayed four-square band, faces separated by one square are opposite.
3. For other nets, mentally fold or track face normals; do not apply a row shortcut to arbitrary bent paths.

### Dice rule
On a standard die, opposite faces sum to 7. If top is 3, bottom is 4.

## 6. Matrix problems

### Method
1. Look at rows first: what changes from left to right?
2. Then columns: what changes from top to bottom?
3. Check if the rule is additive (combine shapes) or transformational (rotate, flip).
4. Apply the rule to the missing cell.
5. Verify against both row and column rules.

### Common rules
- Rotation by 90° each step.
- Number of sides increases by 1.
- Fill alternates.
- Position moves clockwise.
- Shape is combined with the previous shape.
- Elements are added or removed.

## 7. Odd-one-out

Compare across all options:
- Number of sides
- Symmetry
- Open vs. closed
- Straight vs. curved
- Fill
- Orientation
- Number of intersections

The odd one differs on exactly one relevant dimension. Do not choose based on a trivial difference if all options differ trivially.

## 8. Worked examples

### S-EX-01 — Rotation
**Question:** An arrow points north. It is rotated 90° clockwise. Which direction does it point?
A) North B) East C) South D) West
**Answer:** B) East.

### S-EX-02 — 180° rotation
**Question:** Which letter results from rotating lowercase `b` by 180°?
A) `p` B) `d` C) `q` D) `b`
**Answer:** C) `q`.

### S-EX-03 — Corner movement
**Question:** A square has a dot in the top-left corner. The square is rotated 90° clockwise. Where is the dot?
A) Top-left B) Top-right C) Bottom-right D) Bottom-left
**Answer:** B) Top-right.

### S-EX-04 — Paper folding
**Question:** A square sheet is folded in half twice, then a small hole is punched through all layers away from folds and edges. Assume unfolded holes do not overlap. How many separate holes appear?
A) 1 B) 2 C) 4 D) 8
**Answer:** C) 4.

### S-EX-05 — Cube
**Question:** A standard die shows 3 on top. What is on the bottom?
A) 2 B) 3 C) 4 D) 5
**Answer:** C) 4 (opposite faces sum to 7).

### S-EX-06 — Mirror
**Question:** A lowercase `p` is reflected in a vertical mirror. What does it look like?
A) `p` B) `q` C) `d` D) `b`
**Answer:** B) `q`.

### S-EX-07 — Series
**Question:** A shape rotates 45° clockwise each step. After 4 steps, how many degrees has it rotated in total?
A) 90° B) 135° C) 180° D) 225°
**Answer:** C) 180°.

### S-EX-08 — Matrix
**Question:** In a 3×3 matrix, the number of sides increases by 1 across each row: 3, 4, 5. The second row starts at 4: 4, 5, 6. The third row starts at 5. What is the last cell?
A) 5 B) 6 C) 7 D) 8
**Answer:** C) 7.

## 9. Practice drill (8 items, 6 minutes)

1. An arrow points west. Rotate 180°. Direction?
2. Rotate `p` 180°. What letter?
3. A dot is at bottom-right. Rotate the square 90° counter-clockwise. Where is the dot?
4. Fold a square in half three times; punch once through all layers. Holes?
5. A standard die shows 5 on top. Bottom?
6. `d` reflected in a vertical mirror looks like?
7. A shape rotates 30° clockwise each step. Total after 6 steps?
8. Matrix row rule: 2, 4, 6; second row 3, 5, 7; third row 4, 6, ?.

**Answers:**
1. East.
2. `d`.
3. Top-right (bottom-right moves to top-right under 90° counter-clockwise).
4. 8.
5. 2.
6. `b`.
7. 180°.
8. 8.

## 11. Common mistakes

- Tracking too many features at once.
- Confusing rotation with reflection.
- Forgetting that a 180° rotation is not a mirror image.
- Miscounting layers after folding.
- Assuming a cube net has a fixed layout; check the actual net.
- Choosing an odd-one-out based on a feature that varies across all options.
- Spending more than 30 seconds on a spatial item.

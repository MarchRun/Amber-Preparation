## 2. What the company says it does

Ambersof describes itself as helping enterprises transform how they work through:

- Artificial intelligence
- Intelligent automation
- Data
- Enterprise technology

It combines technology expertise with business-process understanding. Its stated goal is to go "beyond digitization" so organisations can operate smarter, make better decisions, and adapt.

Its services span:

- AI-powered applications
- Custom software development
- Oracle solutions
- Odoo ERP implementation
- Microsoft Dynamics 365 implementation
- Intelligent stockpile monitoring
- IT blueprint/roadmap consulting
- Talent services
- Its own product, FOOMA

It states that it has delivered projects across Indonesia, Singapore, New Zealand, and the United States.

**Interpretation:** This is a consultancy with a product arm, not a single-product SaaS company. Revenue likely comes from implementation projects, consulting, talent placement, and FOOMA licensing/services. The exact revenue mix is not public.

## 3. Positioning

### Stated positioning
"Talent and Technology" — combining people and engineering to create sustainable, smart solutions. The company emphasises practical solutions, speed, quality, and a modern agile approach compared with large consulting firms.

### Competitive space
- Mid-market enterprise IT consulting in Indonesia and Southeast Asia.
- ERP implementation (Oracle, Odoo, Microsoft Dynamics 365).
- Custom software and system integration.
- Talent outsourcing/staff augmentation.
- Niche AI/computer-vision solutions (stockpile monitoring, field inspection).

### Likely differentiators
- Regional delivery experience (Indonesia + Singapore + NZ + US).
- Multi-platform ERP capability rather than a single vendor.
- Combination of consulting and hands-on development.
- Own product (FOOMA) that can be bundled with services.

These are inferences from public positioning, not verified competitive advantages.

## 7. Why this company might appeal to a fresh graduate

Evidence-based reasons:

1. **Breadth of exposure.** A generalist programmer can touch Java, PHP, Angular, .NET, Flutter, ERP integrations, and AI projects.
2. **Consulting learning curve.** Client-facing work builds business analysis and communication skills early.
3. **International exposure.** Delivery across four countries implies English use and exposure to different working styles.
4. **Small-team responsibility.** A ~50-person firm likely gives juniors more visible ownership than a large enterprise.
5. **Stated learning culture.** The ad explicitly values research, asking for help, feedback, and growth.

These are reasons to say in an interview, but they must be tied to Marchel's actual goals, not flattery.

## 8. Risks and critical questions

| Question | Why it matters | What to listen for |
|---|---|---|
| What will a fresh graduate actually work on? | The ad lists five stacks; reality may be maintenance work | Specific project examples, mentoring |
| Is there structured onboarding? | Small consultancies vary widely | Named mentor, first-90-day plan |
| Is the contract renewable? | Advertised as contract | Conversion criteria, review cycle |
| How is utilisation measured? | Consultancies bill by project | Reasonable expectations, learning time |
| Will English be used daily? | Company claims international delivery | Client-facing exposure |
| What is the tech stack for this cohort? | Avoid learning five stacks superficially | One or two primary stacks |

Ask at most two of these in the interview. Do not interrogate.

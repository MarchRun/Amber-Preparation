## 2. Success definition

A successful 2 November means:

1. CCAT: practise toward 40 substantive attempts and 30 correct on these original drills. These are personal training targets, not employer thresholds or calibrated predictions. Criteria's published norm-sample mean is 24.2; these homemade mocks are not normed.
2. Business case: clarify requirements before solving, produce a structured answer, and state trade-offs.
3. English: speak in complete but simple sentences; ask for clarification when needed.
4. Honesty: never claim Java/.NET/Flutter production experience that does not exist; frame them as learning targets with evidence of transferable skills.
5. Logistics: join early, camera on, stable audio, materials ready.

These are preparation targets, not promises. Amber's actual cut-off is unknown.

## 7. Decision rules during the assessment

1. If instructions are unclear, ask one precise question: "Could you confirm whether you want a high-level design or working code?"
2. If a task is too large, state assumptions and deliver a thin end-to-end slice.
3. If asked about unknown technology, say: "I have not used it in production. My closest experience is X. I would learn it by Y."
4. If time is short in CCAT, guess and move on. There is no wrong-answer penalty.
5. If the examiner challenges a design, do not defend blindly. Restate the constraint, then adjust.

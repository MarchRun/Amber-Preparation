# Company Culture

**Purpose:** What Amber says about how it works, and how to signal fit without pretending.
**Created:** 31 October 2026
**Last updated:** 31 October 2026
**Research status:** Public values and job ads; no reliable employee reviews found
**Confidence:** Medium for stated culture; low for lived culture

---

## 1. Stated values

| Value | Wording | Behavioural translation |
|---|---|---|
| Passionate | "Strong belief and efforts are the base to achieve long term success" | Show curiosity, initiative, persistence |
| Fast-paced | "Swift in handling new challenges and producing new ideas" | Prioritise, communicate blockers early, deliver iteratively |
| Innovative | "Constantly pursue innovation of our products and services" | Suggest improvements with evidence, not novelty for its own sake |
| Holistic problem solving | "Systematic and creative thinking" | Consider people, process, data, technology |
| Quality | "Well-proven solutions with quality and a modern approach" | Test, document, avoid shortcuts that create debt |
| Team over stars | "We don't need superstars, we need super team" | Credit collaborators; ask for help; share context |
| Brain and heart | "Harmony between logic and empathy" | Explain decisions with both rigor and user impact |
| Learning | "Improve knowledge and enhance competence" | Show a learning method, not just a list of courses |

## 2. What the job ad reveals about culture

The Fresh Graduate Programmer ad explicitly values:

1. **Passion for programming** — not just credential collection.
2. **Basic concepts over superficial SQL** — depth matters.
3. **Willingness to research** — "Oppa Google is always there to help."
4. **Judgment about when to ask** — independence plus collaboration.
5. **Growth mindset and feedback** — coachability.
6. **Common sense** — practical problem solving.
7. **Brain and heart** — technical plus interpersonal.

**Interpretation:** They are selecting for *learnability and judgment*, not a finished specialist. This is good news for a fresh graduate who can demonstrate structured learning.

## 4. How to demonstrate fit

### Show learning velocity
> "When I had to integrate RFID attendance data, I had never worked with that data shape. I mapped the payload, built a validation layer, and tested with sample scans before connecting it to the attendance records."

### Show judgment about asking
> "I try a 20-minute rule: check documentation, reproduce the issue, then ask with a specific question and what I have tried."

### Show team credit
> "The RFID hardware was handled by a separate IoT engineer. My ownership was the web application and the data integration."

### Show client awareness
> "For an internal monitoring system, the users were not just technical. I had to make the status and reports understandable to non-technical staff."

### Show feedback orientation
> "In my internship, code review feedback taught me to separate business logic from controller code. I now structure handlers more carefully."

## 5. Questions that reveal culture (ask two maximum)

1. "How does the team support a fresh graduate's first three months?"
2. "What does a typical project team look like — who owns requirements, development, and testing?"
3. "How do you balance client deadlines with code quality and learning time?"
4. "What separates a fresh graduate who succeeds here from one who struggles?"

These are respectful and signal maturity.

---

## 6. Culture risks to probe gently

| Risk | Question | What a good answer sounds like |
|---|---|---|
| Sink-or-swim onboarding | "Is there a named mentor or buddy?" | Specific onboarding plan |
| Unpaid overtime | "How are project peaks managed?" | Planned, bounded, compensated |
| Vague role | "What would my first project likely be?" | Concrete examples |
| Contract precarity | "What is the path from contract to permanent?" | Clear review criteria |
| Multi-stack dilution | "Will I go deep in one stack or rotate?" | Honest, structured answer |

Do not ask all of these. Choose based on the conversation.

---

## 7. What not to say

- "I want to work here because I need a job." (Honest but poorly framed.)
- "I heard you work with big clients, so it will look good on my CV." (Self-focused.)
- "I can learn anything in a week." (Unbelievable.)
- "I only want frontend." (Contradicts the generalist ad.)
- "I don't like legacy code." (Most consultancy work involves it.)
- "I prefer working alone." (Contradicts "super team".)

---

## 8. Culture-fit answer template

> "I work best in a team where I can ask questions early and still own my part. In my internship, I was the backend developer in a four-person team, so I had to communicate dependencies clearly and not silently block others. I also like environments where people review each other's work, because that is how I improved fastest. From what I read about Amber, the mix of client projects and learning support seems like a good fit for how I work."

Adapt with real examples. Keep it under 45 seconds.
